# Prior art for rigid woven Hadamard matrices

**Record date: 23 November 2026.** Two bounded searches on this date addressed different questions. The four-group review compared the construction, its orders, invariants and counts with named predecessors. The conclusion search looked for the stronger square-order result, in particular a typical minimal *matrix* automorphism group in the classical weaving ensemble. The record below merges both searches. Source-access labels report what the reviewers examined. Bibliographic lookups for this compilation supplement their citations. They do not upgrade a metadata or abstract reading to a full-text review.

**Finding.** Meisner published the square construction, Craigen published the weaving formula, and earlier authors counted many design, graph and matrix classes. Rigid regular matrices also occur in Spence's order-36 catalogue. The reviews found no published theorem establishing typical recovery, signed rigidity and the stated fixed-input count together for Meisner's square ensemble. They found no matching published finite rigid-regular lower bound for the four-group input at 1600, and no statement of the rank bound of Theorem 8.1, of Proposition 8.2 or of Corollary 8.4. These are results of the searches documented below. Historical priority and a global extremal count remain open.

**Provenance.** The handoff revises the numerical comparisons and the status of the separation argument in the earlier novelty review. Its current figures and status govern this record. ## Comparisons used in the manuscript

1. **Counting convention.** Matrix equivalence allows independent signed row and column permutations. Side-preserving rigidity means that only the simultaneous scalar signs stabilize the matrix. When a count also identifies transposes, that extra identification costs at most two. At order $N$, fixing the normalizing row and column produces an ordinary Hadamard design on $N-1$ points. Its automorphism group is the stabilizer of that choice in the effective matrix group. Each matrix class gives at most $N^2$ isomorphism types of these designs before transpose. Every ordinary design obtained from a rigid matrix is rigid, and non-equivalent matrices give non-isomorphic designs. For Menon designs from regular signings, the analogous upper bound per signed class for fixed block size is $2^{N-1}$, before duality. [Conversion arguments](reviews/four-group/credit-count.md)
2. **Merchant's precise comparison.** Theorem 5.2 assumes an ordinary Hadamard design of order $n>2$. It produces at least $(16n-2)!/(2^{10}n^3)$ mutually non-isomorphic rigid ordinary Hadamard designs of order $8n$. The output matrix order is $N=32n$, the input matrix order is $4n=N/8$, and the designs have $N-1$ points. Merchant's output designs are tensor-indecomposable, with one good block and no good points. The logarithm of his lower bound has leading term $\tfrac12N\log N$. On the intersection $N=m^2$ where **both $H(m)$ and $H(m^2/8)$ exist**, the draft's asymptotic corollary for rigid ordinary designs doubles this leading term to $N\log N$. Here $m$ tends to infinity through admissible orders. This compares the lower bounds for the shared design conclusion. Merchant's additional structural properties are specific to his theorem. [Merchant, Theorem 5.2](https://emis.de/ft/43704) · [Square result, M5](reviews/meisner/MEISNER-RIGIDITY.md)
3. **Classical input and modern deductions.** The square construction is Meisner's Theorem 1 with $v=1$, also Craigen's Theorem 1 with full pattern $J_m$. Meisner's published Theorem 3 counts a different doubling family. Applying the draft's gauge and counting analysis to the signed square ensemble gives the two-term exponent for rigid classes only asymptotically. At 1600, the reading-independent lower bound from the square comparison is **greater than $10^{1268}$** Bush-type classes without a rigidity condition. The four-group proof gives **greater than $10^{1514}$** rigid regular classes, each with a Bush-type representative. The square has about $10^{4186}$ distinct labelled outputs against about $10^{3841}$ for four groups. At equal admissible orders its class count exceeds the four-group fixed-input count by a factor $2^{9N/8+o(N)}$ at the level of the two asymptotic estimates. The two finite lower bounds do not order the actual class counts. The exact gauge fibres are for unregularized matrices. Whole-point sign changes give $2^{8h-1}$ distinct four-group raw matrices within one class. The reported exact enlargement of the labelled fibre after deterministic regularization by this same factor is **UNAUDITED (F12)**. Passing to a signed-equivalent regular representative preserves the class counts.
