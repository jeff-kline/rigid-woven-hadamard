## Geometry and invariance

For a flag on a line in group i, the three external joins reach the other three physical points of that line. Its internal join reaches the group-i point of the image line. Conversely, for any opposite group every port is represented once among the h points of that group. At a probe group external to both specified points, two distinct points in the same group never share a port, and two in different groups share a port only at their common line's probe point. The involution gives the identical assertions with sides reversed. Thus at least h−1 of the h points of any such probe group separate the given points.

On one opposite fibre the entrywise product of two rows is, up to an overall sign, the product of the two indicated local basis columns there. This product is constant for equal ports, and is balanced for distinct ports. This proves the test that recovers either partition from the other. Row sign changes multiply any whole row product by a single sign, and column signs cancel. Coordinate permutations carry the constancy tests with them.

## Recovery A

Each port of a fixed row point occurs at 4h coordinates. Consequently the predetermined local quartet lifts at every such point with correlation exactly 4hD, in absolute value. For a quartet with an odd occupancy at some point, conditioning outside its port signs leaves a sum of h independent fair signs times coefficients of absolute value at most 4h. The two-sided sign-sum bound at 2h² gives 2 exp(−h/8). For occupancy 2+2 the row-pair port word at the first point is a uniformly permuted balanced word independent of all coefficients after exposing the other data. The balanced event for independent fair signs has probability at least 1/(h+1). This exhausts mixed occupancies, so the stated union bound suffices.

When no mixed quartet passes the threshold, every collected row-pair product is constant on each column fibre. To establish separation, fix two column points and use a fixed pair from the predetermined threshold quartet at each of at least h−1 separating probe points. Each balanced port word independently agrees on the two relevant labels with probability (h−2)/(2(h−1)). This test is unconditional, since those true quartets always pass. Thus the two exceptional-event bounds can be added with no conditioning penalty. The resulting lists recover the column partition up to relabelling.

A single decoder can dispense with advance knowledge of which local-basis case holds: check whether any quartet reaches the threshold, use A's lists if so, and use B otherwise. The bound for mixed threshold quartets did not require A's local hypothesis. In case B, on the complement of that same exceptional event, no quartet reaches the threshold.

## Recovery B

For a true pair, its product is constant on each column point. At that point the two extension rows contribute a signed inner product of two columns of an orthogonal sign basis, hence a multiple of h. For a false pair, select a separating column point and a third source group. Its ports at the chosen column point correspond bijectively to distinct points of the source group. The auxiliary rows therefore are distinct from each other and from the original pair. Their selection can depend on labels while remaining independent of the target port signs.

For c outside a,b, the vector given by the product of columns a,b,c is nonzero and orthogonal to each of those three columns. Its coefficient on some fourth column d must be nonzero because the h columns form a basis. This proves the minimum-degree bound. For an edge cd only the contribution of the chosen column fibre uses its auxiliary signs in this quartet, and there it appears as a signed D times the product of those two signs. The two residues differ by 2D, nonzero modulo h under B. A forest's edge products of independent vertex signs are independent: choose a sign freely at each root and all other vertex signs are forced by the prescribed edge products. Minimum degree one implies at least (h−2)/2 forest edges. The stated exponential false-pair bound and union bound follow uniformly. Signed equivalence preserves every quartet divisibility condition.

## Finite refinement at local order 20

1. Normalize the first of four distinct columns to all ones by row signs, preserving D. The remaining three sign variables are each balanced and pairwise orthogonal. Let t be the number of coordinates with all three positive. Each pair is simultaneously positive in five coordinates. Expanding the product of their three signs as a product of terms twice an indicator minus one gives D=8t−20. If |D|=20, the last column equals the product of the second and third up to sign. The four columns then span the indicators of the four sign cells of the second and third columns, each of size five. A fifth column orthogonal to all four would have zero sign sum on each odd cell. This is impossible. Thus D belongs to {−12,−4,4,12}. In particular 20 does not divide 2D for any four distinct labels.
2. The edge graph is consequently complete on 18 vertices and a spanning tree has 17 edges. The modular argument from B holds also when |D|=12 because only the two residues need to be distinct. Each false physical row pair passes with probability at most 2⁻¹⁷.
3. The graph on all 1600 rows contains the 80 true twenty-cliques. A second partition into twenty-cliques requires at least two new blocks meeting multiple old blocks. If only one new block were mixed, the other 79 would each exhaust a distinct old block, leaving a whole old block. Every mixed new block contains at least 19 cross pairs, by choosing a nonempty proper old-block intersection of size k and counting k(20−k) crossing pairs. Different new blocks give disjoint sets of pairs. Hence at least 38 erroneous edges are necessary.
4. The count of possible cross pairs is 20² times binom(80,2), yielding expected erroneous edges at most 1,264,000/131,072 < 9.65. Markov bounds the probability of a competing partition by 1,264,000/(131,072·38), less than 0.254. With probability greater than 0.746 the intrinsic graph has a unique partition into twenty-cliques, which recovers the row fibres. The deterministic opposite-side test restores the column fibres on that same event.
