# Bush-type and square-construction credit audit

## Fixed lines give Bush blocks

Use the definition allowing nonsymmetric matrices. At each fixed line the four row point fibres and four column point fibres form sixteen constant tiles in the regular representative. Their signs form $S=(I+K)\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1)$. The regularizing signs cancel the fixed local columns on both ends. This order-four sign matrix has one negative in each row and each column. Split its four rows into two pairs. Assign each pair the two columns avoiding its two negatives. This produces two diagonal all-one blocks of order $2h$. The two remaining blocks on this fixed line have zero margins, as each of their two-by-two sign patterns has one negative per row and column. Between *different* fixed lines, each individual tile uses a nonfixed port at both ends. Its left and right factors, multiplied coordinatewise by the respective fixed columns, are balanced by orthogonality. Each tile therefore has both margins zero. The $h$ fixed lines give $2h$ Bush blocks. This works for every choice of port labellings and independent local Hadamards, and uses independent physical row and column reorderings. The unsigned design from the negative entries is a Menon design with parameters $(4h^2,2h^2-h,h^2-h)$. Scalar signed rigidity implies trivial design automorphism group.

## Comparators

- Bonato, Holzmann and Kharaghani §2, Theorem 1, reproduce a theorem credited to Kharaghani (1985), using a normalized Hadamard of order $m$, the projectors $c_i c_i^T$, and a symmetric constant-diagonal Latin square. With $m=2h$ supplied by doubling, all current orders already have their symmetric witnesses. Their terminology includes symmetry. Kamali, Kharaghani and Khosrovshahi explicitly allow nonsymmetric Bush matrices and report finite lower bounds at orders 64 and 144. I verified their abstract and definition but did not verify their numerical totals. Kharaghani's 1986 paper Corollary 3 attributes the tensor $A^T\otimes A$ Bush equivalence to W. D. Wallis. Muzychuk–Xiang give symmetric witnesses at $4m^4$ for odd $m$ from reversible group difference sets. [Bonato et al., §2](https://math.ryerson.ca/~abonato/papers/3echw2.pdf) · [Kamali et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0378-3758(01)00318-4) · [Kharaghani, Corollary 3](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1446788700028044) · [Muzychuk–Xiang](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0501026)
- Meisner (1992), §§2–3, Theorems 1–2, with $v=1, a=m$, already permits independent signed and permuted bases on both sides of a square. At $(i,j)$ use the outer product of the port column of the row basis and the port row of the column basis, using the all-one column and row for $i=j$. This gives Bush blocks of order $m$, each of rank one. Craigen's (1995) Theorem 1, with pattern $J_m$, also gives this exact formula. Meisner's Theorem 3 separately proves a signed class bound $s+n-1$ at order $4^n v$ given $s$ order-$v\ge4$ regular seeds of distinct characteristic numbers. One structural distinction is that the current four-group port occurs in four separate tiles, and its proven Bush blocks are assembled from four smaller tiles. A distinction between specified partitions is insufficient to prove inequivalence to all Meisner matrices. [Meisner, pp. 235–239](https://www.bdim.eu/item?fmt=pdf&id=RLIN_1992_9_3_4_233_0) · [Craigen, §2](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcd.3180030103)
- Fon-Der-Flaass Construction 1, spelled out by Cameron and Stark, applies to the affine design of sign bipartitions of the normalized Hadamard columns. It permits independent assignments of parallel classes to neighbours at every vertex and independent bijections of the two half-sets on each unordered edge. The resulting matrix is symmetric Bush, with one rank-one tile per edge. Cameron and Stark's Theorem 1.1 counts graph isomorphism types for its random Paley subfamily. Muzychuk §3 and Proposition 3.5 already count the larger relabelled graph family. Substituting $v=m,\ p=m-1,\ n=2,\ s=1$, and the complete graph on $m$ points, (8) gives numerator $((m-1)!)^{m-1}2^{\binom m2}$ and denominator $(m^2)^m\prod_{i=2}^m|\mathrm{Aut}(B_i)|$. Each design automorphism preserves the set of local projective Hadamard columns, ensuring log automorphism order $O(\log^2m)$ by a distinguishing set of row coordinates. Hence logarithm at least $\frac12 N\log N-O(N)$ for $N=m^2$. This rate survives transfer to signed equivalence. For symmetric $H$ and symmetric $P H Q^T$ set $T=P^TQ$. Then $HT^T=TH$, and $(T,T^T)$ stabilizes $H$. The same projective-column argument bounds this group by $exp(O(\log^2 N))$. For fixed $T$, conjugation of $HT^T$ by $P$ accounts for at most $2^N$ graph isomorphism types after discarding the permutation part. These rate and transfer statements are deductions here. ## The full counting rate already follows from the square construction

This is an audit deduction from Meisner's printed construction. Fix the same normalized $H(m)$ at each point, fix its all-one port on the diagonal, and independently permute the other $m-1$ columns or rows at each of the $2m$ point occurrences. There are $((m-1)!)^{2m}$ labellings. In a fixed output matrix guess the image of one whole row fibre of another labelled output under equivalence. There are at most $N^m$ guesses. On these rows projective equality of column restrictions partitions all columns into the $m$ true opposite fibres relative to that labelling, because every port occurs once and the $m$ column directions are distinct. One such column fibre in turn reveals all row fibres. These tests are invariant under arbitrary signed permutations. Once both partitions are given, there are at most $(m!)^2$ assignments of fibre names. Relative to an assigned fibre, choose one reference output labelling for these partitions and names. Every other labelling of the fixed output with that assignment restricts to a signed row or column permutation of its local projective frame relative to the reference. Each local port permutation thus has at most $a_m$ choices, where $a_m=(2m)^{\lceil2\log_2m\rceil+2}$ or the finite bound $4(m)_3((m/4)!)^4$ from FINITE-1600. No port signs need be counted in this unsigned subensemble. The resulting bound is

$$
 \#\text{classes}\ \ge\frac{((m-1)!)^{2m}}{2N^m(m!)^2a_m^{2m}},
 \qquad \log\#\text{classes}=N\log N+O(N).
$$

The factor two allows transposition. At $m=40$ the floor of this bound using the finite $a_m$ has 952 decimal digits (3,161 bits). Thus more than $10^{951}$ Bush-type classes at order 1,600 already follow from one normalized Hadamard in Meisner's construction; no rigidity condition was imposed. This comparative bound is not claimed as a previously published theorem. No corresponding generic rigidity and nonselfduality theorem was located in the checked sources.

## Side-reversing equivalence

Apply the same intrinsic decoder separately to $H$ and $H^T$. The latter has $K^T$, with the same geometry and arbitrary fixed coefficient signs, and transposed local input roles. Both decoder-failure probabilities tend to zero. On success, every side-swapping equivalence maps whole row fibres to true column fibres and vice versa. Each choice of the two point maps forces all port maps. Expose the row labellings. At each independent column point a projective intertwiner between the two prescribed local bases is either impossible or represents a coset of the projective permutation group, of order at most $a$. The unconditional probability bound for any compatible fibre-respecting side swap is $((4h)!)^2(a/h!)^{4h}$. This tends to zero. A matrix equivalent to a symmetric matrix is equivalent to its transpose, so this corollary separates almost all outputs from *every* symmetric construction, including symmetric Bush and graph switching constructions. The conclusion leaves general nonsymmetric Meisner, Craigen, and skew-Bush competitors open.

At $h=20$, the additional side-swap chance is less than $[80^2a_{20}/20!]^{80}<(1/64)^{80}$. Subtract the two decoder failures and one moving-point failure to obtain $1-2f_{20}-g_{20}-(1/64)^{80}>0.45$. The pointwise-fixed kernel proof already applies. Rigidity, nonselfduality and Bush regularity therefore hold jointly for more than 45% at this order.

## Follow-up on B6: complete Meisner separation at order 1,600

The finite modular invariant settles B6 at this order. Meisner's Theorem 1 has entries $Z_{(i,e,b),(j,r,c)}=F^i_{ej}G^j_{ir}M^{ij}_{bc}$ and order $a^2v$. Two rows with equal first index $i$ lie in one clique of the row graph defined by divisibility of every four-row extension by $a$. Indeed, in each fixed $(j,c)$ sum first over $r$. The two factors from these rows give $(G^j_{ir})^2=1$. The two other rows with first indices $k,l$ contribute the dot product of rows $k,l$ of $G^j$, hence zero or $a$. All other factors are signs independent of $r$. This works even when the other rows repeat a first index. The column proof sums over $e$ using columns of $F^i$. [Meisner, p. 236](https://www.bdim.eu/item?fmt=pdf&id=RLIN_1992_9_3_4_233_0)

At order 1,600, $a$ divides 40. The orders 5 and 10 cannot supply $H(a)$; 8 would require $H(25)$. The choices 20 and 40 give respectively row cliques of sizes 80 and 40 in the graph defined by divisibility by 20. All macro cliques simultaneously split into 20-cliques, with multiple possible partitions even in just one macro. The column graph does likewise. Each output passing the finite uniqueness test has a unique row partition into 20-cliques, excluding equivalence to these Meisner outputs or their transposes.

For $a=4$, the eight signed rows of every $H(4)$ are exactly the eight sign vectors with a fixed parity of the number of negative signs: orthogonal rows differ in two coordinates and negation changes four. Thus multiplication by any chosen balanced sign vector $d$ permutes the signed rows and induces a signed permutation $U_i$ of physical local rows with $U_iF^i=F^i\operatorname{diag}(d)$. For $a=2$ the signed rows are the entire sign cube, and the same conclusion holds. Apply $U_i$ on $e$ independently of $b$ and multiply all columns in macro $j$ by $d_j$. These two operations stabilize $Z$ and the column operator is nonscalar. Signed rigidity excludes these choices and their transposes. Meisner §6 also gives explicit involutions for $a=2$.

This exhausts all $a>1$; $a=1$ merely takes the target matrix as input. The more-than-70% finite class in FINITE-1600 therefore lies outside the entire nontrivial Meisner construction up to signed equivalence and transpose, including all its nonsymmetric specializations. This strengthens B6 at order 1,600 only. No asymptotic disjointness from Meisner and no blanket disjointness from all other nonsymmetric constructions follows here.
