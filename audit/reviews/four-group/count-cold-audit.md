## Point stabilizers and class multiplicities

For a fixed geometry the partition of the opposite points into the four neighbors of each port is deterministic. Distinct projective basis directions distinguish these classes. Once the two point permutations are specified, these classes force the port maps in any possible lift. This remains true when corresponding samples have different labellings. All probability bounds on prescribed point permutations can thus be taken unconditionally and added to the probability of failed intrinsic recovery.

For a local basis, a pair of projective columns collides on a sample of $r$ row indices with probability $2^{1-r}$, by orthogonality. The stated $r$ makes the expected number of collisions less than one. Recording the signed row preimages at the selected coordinates determines every transformed signed column, since restrictions distinguish projective columns and the entries have unit magnitude. This gives the stated $(2h)^r$ bound. For each moved cycle, reveal one port permutation freely and then track the successors. The newly revealed independent uniform permutation is in a set of size at most $a$ determined by its predecessor. No closing-edge probability is needed. The sum of cycle lengths minus one per cycle is at least half the total support. Choosing and ordering the support gives the upper bound $(8h)^s$, including permutations of both point sets. The geometric sum over $s\ge2$ tends uniformly to zero.

For an automorphism fixing the points, each external nonzero flag join forces equality of the corresponding multipliers. Connectivity of the external bipartite $K_{4,4}$ minus its matching forces agreement on both sides of each physical line. At any point a nonconstant multiplier is the signed coordinatewise product of two different rows of the local basis. It is balanced. Under independent port permutations its chance of agreeing across the two sides is at most $(2h)^2/\binom{h}{h/2}$ after taking all candidate words. Scalar multipliers at all points propagate through the nonzero tiles to a single global scalar. Simultaneous negation is always an automorphism. A signed regularization conjugates the side-preserving automorphism groups.

For each equivalent good target labelling, choose its point map and one of at most $a$ relative port permutations per point. Each of its $8h$ points allows at most $2^h$ signs. Counting labellings in this manner already accounts for duplicate matrices. A transpose identifies at most two side-preserving equivalence classes. Thus the displayed count follows. Stirling's formula gives $8h\log(h!)=N\log N+O(N)$, while the logarithmic loss from the point maps and the local projective symmetries is $O(h\log^2 h)=o(N)$. The lower bound counts rigid classes with regular representatives and makes no claim that side exchange itself is impossible.

## Order 1600

1. A closed quartet of rows or columns in order 20 would, after normalizing one member, split the coordinates into the four joint sign classes of the next two, each of size five. The fourth member would be their signed product. Orthogonality of a fifth vector to the quartet forces its four cell sums to vanish, impossible on odd cells. A nonconstant multiplier in the pointwise kernel would act by a fixed-point-free involution on the projective local rows. Two disjoint transpositions would give a closed quartet. Thus each kernel word is scalar, deterministically.
2. For any distinct quartet, use the four size-five cells from its first three members after normalization. Orthogonality forces the fourth member's cell sums to be $t,-t,-t,t$ in joint-sign order, with $t$ odd. The fourth sum is $4t$. Closed quartets have been excluded, so its values are $\pm4,\pm12$. Every pair of auxiliary ports contributes an edge in the false-pair divisibility test, and the two possible edge-product values differ by twice the fourth sum, nonzero modulo 20. The complete graph on 18 auxiliary ports thus yields 17 independent forest constraints. The number of false row pairs is $\binom{1600}{2}-80\binom{20}{2}=1{,}264{,}000$. Their expected number of passing tests is at most $1{,}264{,}000/2^{17}=9.6435546875$. Any second partition into passing 20-cliques would have at least two mixed blocks. Each mixed block requires at least 19 distinct false edges, minimizing at a $19+1$ split. Markov therefore bounds failure of recovery on the row axis by $9.65/38$; the geometry then gives the column axis.
3. Three fixed rows partition the projective columns into four ratio-sign patterns of size five. An induced projective permutation is specified by the ordered distinct source rows mapping to these coordinates, their two relative signs, and bijections within the four cells. Hence the number of **induced permutations** is at most $a=4(20\cdot19\cdot18)(5!)^4=5{,}673{,}369{,}600{,}000$. Global signed negation drops out here. This image bound is all that the moved-cycle and multiplicity arguments require.
4. With $R=160\sqrt{a/20!}<0.24434$, the moved-support sum is at most $R^2/(1-R)<0.07901$. Combining this with $9.65/38$ gives good proportion greater than $2/3$. The transpose-identified count is therefore strictly greater than
