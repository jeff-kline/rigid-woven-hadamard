# Extending the incidence construction beyond square fibres

23 November 2026. Proof packet v1. Two extensions of the parameter image are compared with independent classical order witnesses.

## A line involution supplies the repair using ordinary Hadamard bases

Use four groups of h points and h² physical lines. Each line meets each group once and every pair of points in distinct groups has a unique common line. Write ℓᵢ(L) for the point of group i on line L. Suppose there is an involution π on the lines such that for each group i the map L ↦ (ℓᵢ(L),ℓᵢ(πL)) lists every ordered pair of its points exactly once. At each point choose a bijection of its h incident lines with the column labels of a real Hadamard of order h. The local row and column bases may be chosen independently as ordinary Hadamards. They need not be unbiased, and h need not be a square.

The 4h² flags may be indexed as (i,L). Let C=K⊗I on these indices for the fixed skew conference K of order four, with rows (0,1,1,1), (−1,0,1,−1), (−1,−1,0,1), (−1,1,−1,0). Let P=I⊗Π, where Π is the permutation matrix of π. Then P is symmetric, C is skew, CP=PC, CCᵀ=3I and PPᵀ=I. Thus M=h(C+P) has MMᵀ=4h²I. Regroup flags by physical point. At each point apply a locally chosen Hadamard F/√h on the left and (G/√h)ᵀ on the right (beforehand label the incident flags for each basis). These orthogonal basis changes preserve the Gram.

Between different groups each point pair has exactly one C entry, from the unique physical line. Within a group each ordered point pair has exactly one P entry, by the property of π. Each transformed block is therefore one signed outer product of two columns with multiplier h/(√h√h)=1. This proves sign entries everywhere. In the old aligned repair an internal tile was a full product FGᵀ/√h; here the involution places just one matched pair of flags in each internal tile. This is the step that removes the square restriction.

**Regularity.** At any point x there is exactly one line L through x with πL also through x, by the diagonal ordered-pair property. Uniqueness and π²=1 imply πL=L. Consequently the fixed lines partition the physical points, one per group on each fixed line. Let d=(−1,1,1,1), so (I+K)d=2·1. As column signs at any point in group i choose dᵢ times the column of its local G indexed by its unique fixed line. The normalized transpose Gᵀ/√h sends these signs to dᵢ√h on this flag and zero elsewhere. On each fixed line M acts as h(K+I); nonfixed lines do not mix with fixed lines. The left basis change therefore produces a vector whose entries at each point are 2h times its corresponding sign column of F. Multiplying rows by that column makes all row sums 2h. If H1=2h·1 and HᵀH=4h²I, then Hᵀ1=2h·1 also. This works for every choice of local ordinary bases and port labellings with this fixed K.

**Supply of incidence data.** On a field of order q take lines labelled by pairs (a,b) and the involution (a,b) ↦ (b,a). At four groups take four distinct projective linear forms ℓᵢ(a,b), one from each of four directions. Distinct directions give unique external joins. Select each direction off the eigendirections of the swap on the dual space: then ℓᵢ and ℓᵢ∘π are independent, giving the internal ordered-pair property. In characteristic two there is one forbidden direction, in odd characteristic there are two. Hence four directions exist at every even prime power q≥4 and odd prime power q≥5. Products of these designs (coordinatewise forms and simultaneous swap, using the same four group names) preserve both bijections. Thus every h with an ordinary H(h) whose prime-power factors in its canonical factorization meet these bounds has all required inputs. For example h=20 uses F₄ and F₅. At F₄ choose infinity, zero, and the two finite slopes other than 0,1. At F₅ choose infinity, zero, 2, 3. Paley at 19 supplies H(20). Multiplication by a Sylvester factor and replacing F₄ by F_{2^{m+2}} supplies h=20·2ᵐ for all m≥0, always nonsquare.

**Strict enlargement.** These last orders are 1600·4ᵐ. Their 2-adic valuation is even, whereas all previous paired orders at even t have odd valuation. For the earlier explicitly supplied wider dyadic family write its fibre size as 4ʲ and its group size as 4ᵃ≤4ʲ. If a=j the order is a power of two. Otherwise its odd part is 1+(k−1)4^{j−a}, k a power of two, so the 2-adic valuation of its odd part minus one is even. The new sequence has odd part 25, and 25−1=24 has odd 2-adic valuation. This proves disjointness from both previous supplied images.

**Density and prior order coverage.** For every prime p≡7 mod 12 put h=p+1. Then 4|h and 3 does not divide h, so the canonical prime-power factors meet the field-size bounds, and Paley gives a real Hadamard of order h. Through order X this subfamily alone has asymptotically √X/(4 log X) distinct orders, by the prime number theorem for this progression up to √X/2−1. All orders of this transfer have the form 4h², giving the upper bound √X/2, hence its distinct-order count is X^(1/2+o(1)). The exponent 1/2 is the largest possible for regular Hadamard orders because every such order is a square. This does not establish positive relative density among square candidates. The previous paired incidence family, even allowing its line size to vary, had count X^(1/4+o(1)); the supplied wider dyadic family adds at most O((log X)³) since its line and group sizes are power-of-two parameters bounded in terms of the order.

Every order of this regular branch already has an independent regular witness. If A is any Hadamard of order h, flatten the sign matrix A to a vector indexed by pairs. Acting with A⊗A on this vector gives the flattened matrix A A Aᵀ=hA. Row and column sign changes by that vector make A⊗A regular with sum h. Tensor with the regular order-four matrix J₄−2I₄. Thus the order expansion cannot close an open regular or ordinary order. The equivalence classes of the new recipe have not been compared to those products; no rigidity, inequivalence or historical priority is asserted. Related prior work includes Kharaghani's Bush-type construction, which likewise gives regular square orders. [Paley](https://doi.org/10.1002/sapm1933121311) · [Bonato, Holzmann and Kharaghani, Theorem 1](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/v8i1r1/pdf)

## A singleton hole keeps the original square-fibre transfer

Fix h=t² with even t and an unbiased pair of order h. Use a Steiner 2-(3h+1,4,1) design with replication h. This design exists at every such t by the exact block-size-four design spectrum (3h+1≡1 mod 12). [Hanani](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177705047). Use one skew order-four conference per line to obtain C on the flags. Now the repair is simply I. The matrix M=hC+tI gives MMᵀ=(3h²+h)I. The original normalized local F,G changes give one signed outer product between distinct points and FGᵀ/t on each diagonal point block. Thus N=h(3h+1). At h=4 the 13-point projective plane over F₃ gives order 52. The Walsh inputs h=4ʲ give an infinite branch.

The Walsh singleton sequence is disjoint from the old paired image by 2-adic valuation. Its valuation is 2j and its odd part minus one has valuation 2j. In the supplied dyadic wider-hole family the power-of-two case cannot match. Otherwise these two valuations are respectively 2(j′+a) and 2(j′−a), unequal for a≥1. The order-52 singleton example is classical Paley II over F₂₅. For j≥2, Craigen's construction gives order 8(1+3·4ʲ) from complex Golay lengths 1 and 3·4ʲ. Sylvester multiplication by 4ʲ/8 gives the singleton order. Thus this infinite comparison produces no previously open ordinary order. Beyond Walsh parameters these order comparisons make no blanket claim. [Craigen, Corollary 11](https://combinatorialpress.com/article/jcmcc/Volume%2015/vol-15-paper%2013.pdf)
