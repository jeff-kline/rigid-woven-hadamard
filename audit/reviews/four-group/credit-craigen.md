# Craigen weaving and signed-group credit

The four-group construction in [CONSTRUCTIONS.md](CONSTRUCTIONS.md) has two exact classical constituents. Each of its sixteen group-pair submatrices is a Craigen weave. Its middle weighing matrix is a monomial representation of an order-four signed-group Hadamard matrix. These identifications apply to arbitrary local bases and labellings.

## The sixteen weaves

Fix row group i and column group j. For i≠j the join pairs the flag on L with the flag on L at the opposite point. For i=j it pairs the flag on L with that on πL. In either case, as the opposite point runs through its group, a fixed point uses each of its h flags exactly once, on both axes. In Craigen's Theorem 1 put the pattern equal to the h-by-h all-ones matrix. For the constituent at row point x take F_x with columns ordered by the opposite points. For the constituent at column point y take G_y transposed with rows ordered by the opposite points. The weave gives exactly this h²-by-h² submatrix, up to the constant sign K_ij for i≠j. Thus all sixteen are themselves Hadamard matrices. Weaving with arbitrary constituents and obtaining independent rank-one outer products by ordering local bases are classical. The standard one-step weave, if applied to a fully occupied 4h-by-4h pattern, requires constituent orders 4h. The displayed point partition has order-h constituents and each direction appears four times. This comparison leaves arbitrary repartitions open. Craigen and Kharaghani also investigated regular woven matrices and the dependence of excess on choices of constituent row sums. [Craigen, Theorem 1](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcd.3180030103) · [Craigen and Kharaghani](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcd.20012)

## Exact signed-group core and the scope of the lift

Use the signed group with elements ±1, ±u, where u is a central involution independent of the distinguished sign. In its signed group algebra take S=K+uI. The adjoint sends u to u and K to −K, giving SS*=4I. In Craigen's real monomial representation terminology, take degree h² on the physical lines, mapping u to Π and −1 to −I. Then ρ(S)=K⊗I+I⊗Π is precisely the sparse middle factor before scaling. Craigen's standard conversion multiplies this on the right by I₄⊗V for a single ordinary H(h²); one can take V=A⊗A. It gives the same order. In the four-group family one instead places two independent families of sparse orthogonal changes on either side, grouped by physical points, with flatness supplied by the one-join property. [Craigen, Theorem 5](https://doi.org/10.1016/0097-3165(95)90002-0) · [Craigen's representation and weighing-matrix formulation, Theorem 4](https://us.ticmeet.com/assets/archivos/d6f1d9b8-d39f-4888-925a-7eb81c4905cc/Craigen_handouts.pdf)

There is a signed-equivalence obstruction for the standard conversion of this particular S. In the group order of the packet take σ=(12)(34), s=(1,−1,−1,1), Q_ab=s_a when b=σ(a) and zero elsewhere. Checking the six independent entries gives QKQᵀ=K. Thus Q⊗I simultaneously permutes and signs rows and columns of ρ(S)(I₄⊗V), preserving it for every V. Rigid four-group outputs have no such extra symmetry. This calculation only excludes this direct common-V substitution and its equivalences.

## A broader block product

McNulty and Weigert's construction uses normalized macroblocks scalar-times U_i* V_j. For such a factorization the normalized product around any rectangle B_ij B_kj* B_kl B_il* is scalar. In the packet write the normalized group blocks as B_ij=X_i T_ij Y_j*, with the two orthogonal line-to-point basis changes and T_ii=Π, T_ij=K_ij I for i≠j. Choose rows 1,2 and columns 1,3. The rectangle gives ±X₁ΠX₁*, which is nonscalar for h>1 because the ordered-pair property forces a moved line. Thus the two-sided scalar block product does not describe these sixteen group blocks in the given partition, even after separate orthogonal changes at each group. Other repartitions and more general controlled block operations are outside this test. [McNulty and Weigert](https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.1057)

The underlying four columns ℓ_i form an ordinary index-one strength-two orthogonal array. The condition on π supplies four additional balanced projections (ℓ_i,ℓ_i∘π) through the same row involution. The ordinary definition of a nested orthogonal array requires a subarray that is itself an orthogonal array. It does not give this row permutation or the signed-basis switches. Arbitrary cross projections (ℓ_i,ℓ_j∘π), i≠j, are not required here. [Mukerjee, Qian and Wu](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0012365X07007078)

## Attribution boundary

The preceding paired incidence family uses unbiased bases in full repair tiles. Its order comparison does not establish priority for this all-rank-one four-group family. The existence of regular H(4h²) from H(h) already follows by regularizing the tensor square and multiplying by a regular H(4). These sources supply neither a proof of intrinsic fibre recovery in the four-group ensemble nor the probability of its minimal signed automorphism group or the leading class count in [REGULAR-RIGIDITY.md](REGULAR-RIGIDITY.md). A claim about those statements should cite the constituent constructions and limit any priority wording to the literature actually checked.

## Order 1600 excludes every nontrivial single real weave

This deduction uses the simultaneous event in the [finite proof](FINITE-1600.md) and the [side-swap calculation](credit-bush.md). Write Γ_t for the graph on rows (or columns) joining two when every extension to four distinct vectors has correlation divisible by t. Signed equivalence preserves these graphs and transpose exchanges the two axes. At h=20, on each axis fewer than 38 edges of Γ₂₀ cross actual fibres of size 20, except with probability at most f₂₀. Requiring that inequality on both axes, point rigidity and absence of side-swapping equivalences costs at most 2f₂₀+g₂₀+64⁻⁸⁰. The joint event has probability greater than 0.45.

A single weave producing a dense real matrix has full pattern, because each zero in the pattern gives an entire zero block. Every constituent is itself dense, since any zero in the outer factors leaves a zero entry. Thus Craigen's Theorem 1 reduces to a weave of a real Hadamards of order b on the row side and b real Hadamards of order a on the column side. Up to transposition take 1<a≤b, ab=1600. The possible (a,b) are (2,800), (4,400), (8,200), (16,100), (20,80), (40,40), by the order restriction on real Hadamards. In row group i and column group j the entry is Fⁱ_ej Gʲ_ir. Two rows of the same i have product independent of r; at fixed j the remaining pair contributes a sum over r of 0 or ±a. Each of the a groups of b rows is therefore a clique in Γ_a. Interchanging the axes gives b cliques of size a in the column graph Γ_b. [Craigen, Theorem 1](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcd.3180030103)

For a=2 or 4 choose a nonconstant word d on the a row groups, with two minus signs when a=4. Multiplication by d preserves the signed column set of each Gʲ. For a=2 the signed columns exhaust the sign cube. For a=4 they exhaust a parity class, since their pairwise Hamming distances are two and there are eight distinct signed columns. A column signed permutation at each j compensates row multiplication by d_i on group i. This is an extra signed automorphism. For a=20 or 40 the row groups are Γ₂₀ cliques of sizes 80 or 40 and have multiple partitions into 20-cliques. The joint event forces a unique 20-clique partition on each side, since a second partition would require at least 38 cross edges.

For a=8 or 16, 20 divides b, so the column groups give a partition into Γ₂₀ cliques of size a. Within a single true 20-fibre the completely contained a-cliques of that partition cover at most 16 points. Thus at least 320 points altogether belong to mixed a-cliques. There must be at least 320/a such cliques, each with at least a−1 edges crossing true fibres. Different cliques in the partition have disjoint edge sets, so the column graph has at least 280 or 300 cross edges respectively, contradicting the joint event. Having the edge bound on both axes excludes the transposed construction as well.

Consequently the certified labellings escape all nontrivial single real Craigen weaves even under arbitrary signed equivalence and transpose. The class bound on this joint event is 0.45(20!)¹⁶⁰/[2(80!)²(5,673,369,600,000)¹⁶⁰], whose base-ten logarithm exceeds 662.80. These classes are also nonselfdual and so exclude every symmetric representative. The assertion concerns a single application of the classical weave; each of the sixteen macroblocks of the constructed matrix remains exactly a classical weave as above.
